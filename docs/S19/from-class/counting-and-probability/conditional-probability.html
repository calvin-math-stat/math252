Counting and Probability

6 Conditional Probability

\(operatorname{p}(A \mid B)\) answers the question: Of the times that \(B\) happens, how often does \(A\) also happen? Common ways this is expressed include

  • The probability of \(A\) given \(B\)
  • The probability of \(A\) conditional on \(B\)
  • The probability of \(A\) if \(B\)
  • The probability that \(A\) happens when \(B\) happens

When \(operatorname{p}(B) \neq 0\), then \[ operatorname{p}(A \mid B) = \frac{operatorname{p}(A \;\cap\;B)}{operatorname{p}(B)} \]

Note: Usually, \(operatorname{p}(A \mid B)\), \(operatorname{p}(B \mid A)\) and \(operatorname{p}(A \;\cap\;B)\) are all different. It is critical to know which of these three applies in a given situation and to use the notation correctly.

6.1 Exercises

A number of school children, some boys and some girls, are asked about their favorite color. The resuls are in the table below.

\ Blue Other
Boy 18 7
Girl 12 8

Suppose we put the names of all the children into a hat and selection one randomly. Consider the following events.

  • \(G\): The selected child is a girl.
  • \(L\): The selected child’s favorite color is blue.

For each of the following, (a) express the probability in words, (b) determine the probability. For conditional probabilities, compute the probabilities two different ways.

  1. \(operatorname{p}(L)\)
  2. \(operatorname{p}(\overline{L})\)
  3. \(operatorname{p}(G)\)
  4. \(operatorname{p}(\overline{G})\)
  5. \(operatorname{p}(L \;\cap\;G)\)
  6. \(operatorname{p}(L \mid G)\)
  7. \(operatorname{p}(G \mid L)\)
  8. \(operatorname{p}(G \mid \overline{L})\)
  9. \(operatorname{p}(\overline{G} \mid L)\)

6.2 Product Rule

Applying a little algebra, we get the following product rule:

\[ operatorname{p}(A \;\cap\;B) = operatorname{p}(A) \cdot operatorname{p}(B \mid A) \]

6.3 Independence

If \(operatorname{p}(A) \neq 0\) and \(operatorname{p}(B) \neq 0\) and \(operatorname{p}(B \mid A) = operatorname{p}(B)\), then we say that \(A\) and \(B\) are independent events.

6.4 Exercises

  1. Show that if \(operatorname{p}(B \mid A) = operatorname{p}(B)\), then \(operatorname{p}(A \mid B) = operatorname{p}(A)\).

  1. The product rule is even simpler when \(A\) and \(B\) are independent. What is it?
  1. Show that \(operatorname{p}(A \;\cap\;B \;\cap\;C) = operatorname{p}(A) \cdot operatorname{p}(B \mid A) \cdot operatorname{p}(C \mid A \;\cap\;B)\).
  1. Analogous rules hold for intersections of more events as well. Write down the rule for the intersection of 4 events.
  1. What is the probability of rolling doubles (two numbers that match) with standard dice? Do this two ways: (a) using the Equally Likely Rule and (b) using the Product Rule.
  1. What is the probability of a five-card flush (all cards the same suit)? Do this two ways: (a) using the Equally Likely Rule and (b) using the Product Rule.
  1. How many people are in this room? What is the probability that two people in this room have the same birthday (month and day)? (Hint: Use the Complement Rule.)

  1. If two 6-sided dice are rolled and the first one is a 5, what is the probability that the sum is 10?
